How it works

What Steam counts
Every click on a link with ?utm_… and what happened next: visits, wishlist adds, purchases and activations. The report is in Steamworks → Marketing & Visibility → UTM Analytics (linked for each project above). Data shows up with about a day’s delay.
Where links are clickable
In YouTube Shorts, TikTok and Reels, links in captions and comments aren’t clickable — only the channel profile / bio link and the story sticker work. In X, Telegram, Discord, VK and Reddit posts and in a long YouTube video’s description, links are clickable.
Automatic link in posts
Write {steam} in a post text or video description — when it’s published, each network gets its own link in its place: the network comes from the post itself, the placement is post, video or shorts, and the campaign is the linked goal’s name (no goal — organic). It needs a selected project with a Steam App ID, otherwise {steam} is simply removed from the text.
The invisible part
Most Shorts viewers come without a link — they type the name into Steam search. UTM shows only part of the effect; look for the rest in Steam traffic (growth in “search” and direct visits) and in wishlist growth after videos.
Naming
One campaign — one slug across all networks, e.g. patch_oct or nextfest_2027. Network and placement are filled in for you (utm_source=tiktok, utm_medium=bio). Cyrillic names are transliterated to Latin, other symbols become _. Use a content tag to tell videos apart within a campaign (trailer_v2).
